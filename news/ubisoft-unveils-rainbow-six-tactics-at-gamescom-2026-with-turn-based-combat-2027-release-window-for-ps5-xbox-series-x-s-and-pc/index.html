Ubisoft Announces Rainbow Six Tactics at Gamescom 2026, Bringing Siege Operators to Turn-Based Combat

The new single-player Rainbow Six game brings Siege Operators, destructible environments, reconnaissance, and squad-based tactical missions into a strategy format.

By Christopher Ivanov
Aug 26, 2026
Summary
  • Ubisoft revealed Tom Clancy’s Rainbow Six Tactics at Gamescom 2026, shifting the series into turn-based strategy.
  • The game features 15 Operators, four-person squads, destructible environments, reconnaissance, and coordinated breach planning.
  • Rainbow Six Tactics launches in 2027 for PS5, Xbox Series X|S, and PC as a single-player experience.

Ubisoft used Gamescom Opening Night Live 2026 to reveal Tom Clancy’s Rainbow Six Tactics, a new single-player strategy game that trades the reflex-heavy firefights of Rainbow Six Siege for turn-based combat, squad management, reconnaissance, and carefully planned breaches.

The game was officially unveiled on August 25 during Geoff Keighley’s Gamescom showcase. Rather than creating another multiplayer variation of Siege, Ubisoft is taking familiar Operators and placing them inside a tactical campaign built around controlling an entire squad.

Rainbow Six Tactics is currently scheduled for 2027 on PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S, and PC. Ubisoft has not announced a specific release date yet.

That shift immediately invites comparisons with games such as XCOM, although simply calling this “Rainbow Six XCOM” misses some of the more interesting ideas Ubisoft showed. Environmental destruction, drones, Operator gadgets, synchronized breaches, and the intelligence-gathering phase of a traditional Rainbow Six operation are being adapted into the strategy format rather than discarded.

Rainbow Six Tactics puts the player in command of Team Rainbow

Instead of controlling a single soldier from a first-person perspective, players take the role of Six, the commander responsible for assembling and directing Rainbow’s Operators.

Ubisoft says the game will feature 15 playable Operators, each carrying individual abilities, specialized gadgets, and their own skill trees. Before deploying, players can choose a squad of up to four Operators based on the objective and available intelligence.

Several familiar Rainbow Six Siege characters have already appeared, including Ash, Glaz, Nøkk, Doc, Montagne, Sledge, and Twitch. Their equipment is being reworked around turn-based systems while retaining the battlefield roles associated with their Siege versions.

That distinction matters. Operator selection in Siege happens before relatively short multiplayer rounds. Tactics can make those choices part of a larger campaign, where squad composition, upgrades, equipment, and potentially the consequences of earlier missions influence what comes next.

Ubisoft’s current description includes:

  • 15 Operators with individual gadgets, abilities, and progression trees
  • Squads containing up to four Operators
  • Mission types such as Hostage Rescue, Bomb Disposal, and Target Elimination
  • More than 45 locations around the world
  • Destructible environments and coordinated breach attacks
  • Headquarters management and Operator training between deployments

Those features have been confirmed directly by Ubisoft rather than inferred from the reveal trailer.

Missions start before anybody fires a shot

One of the smarter choices Ubisoft appears to be making is keeping reconnaissance relevant.

Players can gather intelligence before committing their team, including using familiar Rainbow Six reconnaissance tools to identify threats and determine how an area should be attacked. Mission preparation includes choosing Operators, studying available information, selecting entry points, and deciding how each member of the squad fits into the plan.

Once combat begins, movement and attacks use a turn-based tactical system.

Destructible environments remain an important part of the equation. Walls can be destroyed to produce new lines of sight, eliminate enemy cover, or open an alternative route into a room. That should help Rainbow Six Tactics avoid one of the duller habits of the genre, where maps occasionally become elaborate chessboards covered in indestructible waist-high furniture.

Here, the chessboard can apparently be demolished. Much more appropriate for Rainbow Six.

Breach Planning tries to preserve the Rainbow Six identity

The feature that may separate Tactics from more conventional turn-based strategy games is called Breach Planning.

Players can coordinate several actions across their active squad and then trigger a synchronized assault. Operators can enter through different positions, use gadgets, destroy walls, and attack targets as part of the same planned operation.

Ubisoft describes the system as a way to translate the familiar Rainbow Six breach into turn-based combat without reducing everything to moving characters from one square to another.

The reveal footage suggests that planning can temporarily give way to a coordinated sequence where the operation unfolds in real time. Reconnaissance, preparation, execution, and improvisation therefore appear to form the basic rhythm of a mission.

Creative director Elie Benhamou said the team viewed turn-based combat as compatible with Rainbow Six because the franchise has always revolved around intelligence, planning, specialized Operators, and high-risk decisions. That argument is fairly convincing. The series was obsessed with planning rooms and tactical maps long before Siege turned Rainbow Six into an esports fixture.

Rainbow Six Tactics reconnects with Rainbow Six Vegas

Ubisoft is also digging into older Rainbow Six history.

The commander known as Six is Paul Bishop, the protagonist associated with Rainbow Six Vegas 2. The new game places Bishop in charge of Team Rainbow and connects its story with the existing Rainbow Six Siege timeline.

That gives Tactics an unusual role inside the franchise. Mechanically, it is moving away from the first-person format. Narratively, it is reaching backward toward the series’ previous single-player era while using the Operators and ongoing storyline established by Siege.

GamingTrend reports that the story sits between events from Siege’s Year 10 and Year 11 material, with Rainbow confronting the Keres Legion as the organization rebuilds and returns to active operations.

It is also the first dedicated Rainbow Six single-player campaign of this kind since Rainbow Six Vegas 2 in 2008. That makes the announcement more interesting than another attempt to stretch Siege sideways into a neighboring multiplayer concept.

Headquarters management continues between missions

The strategic layer does not end when an operation is completed.

Back at headquarters, players will be able to train Operators, upgrade the base, strengthen squad capabilities, and prepare for future deployments. Ubisoft has yet to explain how extensive these management systems will become, so comparisons with the campaign layer of XCOM should remain tentative for the moment.

The existence of skill trees and headquarters upgrades suggests that missions form part of an interconnected progression system rather than behaving like isolated tactical puzzles.

Much will depend on how aggressively Ubisoft handles progression. Tactical games live or die on meaningful decisions. Twenty upgrade menus filled with microscopic percentage increases would technically qualify as “depth,” in the same way a tax return technically qualifies as interactive entertainment.

The reveal gives Ubisoft the benefit of the doubt for now.

Ubisoft says Rainbow Six Tactics will avoid live-service monetization

Perhaps the strangest sentence attached to a modern Ubisoft announcement concerns what Rainbow Six Tactics apparently will not contain.

Game Informer reports that Ubisoft says the game is being designed as a strictly single-player title without an in-game store or live-service structure. Other reports from the Gamescom presentation likewise state that additional in-game monetization is not planned.

That does not automatically rule out traditional expansions or post-launch DLC, and Ubisoft has not yet detailed editions or final pricing. Still, positioning Tactics as a self-contained premium campaign separates it rather sharply from the seasonal machinery surrounding Rainbow Six Siege.

For a publisher that rarely sees a menu it cannot imagine attaching another menu to, the restraint is noteworthy.

When does Rainbow Six Tactics release?

Tom Clancy’s Rainbow Six Tactics is scheduled to launch in 2027 for PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S, and PC. The PC version will be available through the Ubisoft Store, Steam, and Epic Games Store, while Ubisoft also lists GeForce Now and Blacknut among its supported platforms.

No Nintendo Switch 2 version has been announced, and Ubisoft has not provided a specific month or release date.

Players can already wishlist the game.

The larger question is whether Ubisoft Paris can make familiar Siege mechanics genuinely matter inside a slower strategy game. Simply placing Ash and Sledge on an isometric map would be an expensive costume change. Turning drones, destruction, Operator combinations, reconnaissance, and synchronized breaches into systems that continually interact with each other would be considerably more interesting.

The first footage suggests Ubisoft understands that distinction.

Rainbow Six Tactics is less about abandoning what made Rainbow Six tactical and more about removing the need to execute those ideas at 120 frames per second while somebody called xXHeadshotLord97Xx screams through a microphone. For players who enjoy Rainbow Six’s planning more than its multiplayer circus, 2027 suddenly has another game worth watching.
